What is your day worth?

From the daily rate to half days and overtime: three simple formulas, worked examples in euros, and one place to write it all down.

1. The daily rate

Start from the monthly pay and the days actually worked that month:

Daily pay = monthly pay ÷ days worked

Example: 1,800 over 22 days worked is 81.82 a day. One detail changes everything: in a month with 21 or 23 working days the same salary gives a different daily rate. Anyone paid by the day or by the hour should never do this sum once and then trust their memory.

2. Hourly pay and half days

Hourly pay = daily pay ÷ hours in a full day

Example: 81.82 ÷ 8 hours = 10.23 an hour.

From there, any part of a day is just a percentage: you do not need to redo the maths each time, only count the hours you worked.

Quick examples (8 hours, 81.82 a day)

8 hours — full day

100% of the daily pay = 81.82.

6 hours — three quarters

75% = 61.37.

4 hours — half day

50% = 40.91.

2 hours — part of a day

25% = 20.46.

1 hour — hourly pay

12.5% = 10.23.

Overtime

3. Hours beyond a full day

Hours worked beyond a full day are usually paid at a multiplier set by your contract or by law: 1.25 and 1.5 are the most common. Multiply the hourly pay by the multiplier, then by the extra hours.

The formula

Hourly pay × multiplier × extra hours = amount.

Example at 1.5

10.23 × 1.5 × 3 hours = 46.04 extra on top of the day.

Please note

These are arithmetic examples, not legal advice: your contract and your country's law decide the real rate.

4. Why log every day

The maths is only right if the numbers are right. Memory fails after two weeks, especially with half days and small expenses: transport, materials, food, phone credit.

Write it down the same day, not at the end of the month: full day, half day or rest day, with money in and money out. Then, when the person paying you does their own count, you have something to compare against instead of an impression.

This matters for anyone paid by the day or by the hour: domestic and care workers, cleaners, construction crews, delivery riders, seasonal farm workers, and anyone whose pay depends on how many days they turned up.

5. A log instead of paper slips

A notebook works, but it gets wet, gets lost, and does not add up. An app on the phone does the same job in two taps and gives you the month's total without opening a calculator.

Frequently asked

Is a half day half pay?
Usually yes, if a full day is 8 hours and the half day is 4. With other hours, work out the share of your own full day.
How do I work out one hour?
Daily pay divided by the hours in a full day: 81.82 ÷ 8 = 10.23.
