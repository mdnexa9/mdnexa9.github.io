Guide · Work time record

A work time record someone else can read.

A record is not a diary and it is not a payslip. It is the thing that answers “which days, how many hours, at what rate, and what arrived” without anyone having to trust your memory. Six fields, one entry a day, and it still makes sense a year later.

What the record is actually for

Three situations, all ordinary: you want to check a payslip without guessing; your employer asks how the month was made up; and someone neutral later needs to see whether the numbers are consistent. The same record answers all three — which is why it should be written in a form a stranger can follow, not in abbreviations you invented under pressure.

That rules out two things people often keep instead: a photo of a handwritten scrap with no month on it, and a total in a notes app with no days behind it. Both show an amount. Neither shows a record.

The six fields

Date. Day, month, year — or a proper date field that sorts correctly. “Tuesday” is not a date.

Type of day. Full day, half day, part of a day, day off, paid leave. Classification causes more disagreements than arithmetic does.

Hours. Start and end time, or the total when times are unreliable. Include the break separately.

Paid or unpaid break. A single flag. Without it, two people can compute two different totals from the same day and both be honest.

Rate applied. The figure agreed for that period. If it changes mid-month, the record shows where.

Amount received and its date. Kept separate from what was earned. The difference between the two is the only number that matters when payment is late.

Expenses you paid yourself — transport, tools, meals away — go in the same month, in their own column, so the month is complete when you export it.

Daily entries, weekly totals

Enter every day, then look at the week. The weekly view is where errors surface: a missing Tuesday, a half day entered as a full day, two shifts merged into one long one. Catching them on Sunday costs nothing; catching them three months later costs the credibility of the whole record.

At the end of the month, export or copy the summary and send it while the month is still recent. A summary sent on the first working day of the next month is a normal administrative message. The same summary sent two months later is a claim.

Keeping it, and sharing it

Keep it longer than you think. Wage claims usually have a time limit counted from the date the pay was due; in many countries that is between one and three years, and sometimes longer for specific claims. Keeping the current year and the previous one is a sensible floor — and ask locally, because the rule is national, not universal.

Keep the original. If an employer signs a weekly summary, photograph it and keep the paper. If confirmation arrives as a message, keep the message, not your retelling of it.

Share summaries, not the whole file. A monthly summary with days and totals is enough for a normal conversation. The full record — with your notes and your own corrections — stays with you until someone with a legitimate reason asks for it.

Keep it in a format you can open later. A CSV or a PDF you can still read in five years beats an account on a service that may not exist. This is the practical argument for records that live on your own device and export cleanly.

Format: paper, sheet or app

Paper is honest and permanent, and the reason it fails is distance: the notebook is at home when the shift ends.

A spreadsheet is the best format for someone at a desk, and the worst for someone moving between sites all day.

A phone app wins because the entry happens where the work does. Pick one with no account requirement, data stored on the device, and an export, so the record is still yours if you change phone or app.

Frequently asked

What belongs in the record?
Date, type of day, hours, paid or unpaid break, the rate applied, and the amount received with its date.
How long should I keep it?
At least as long as the local wage-claim period — often one to three years. Current year plus the previous year is a safe floor.
Is my own record evidence?
It is not a verdict, but a dated record made at the time is far stronger than a reconstruction, especially when it matches messages and transfers.
Should my employer sign it?
If they will, yes. If not, send the monthly summary anyway and keep the sending date.
Does MD Work export the record?
Yes — the monthly summary exports as CSV or as an image, and the data stays on your phone.
